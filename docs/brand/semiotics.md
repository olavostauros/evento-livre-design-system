# Semiotics

How Evento Livre means something when it is not using words or letters.
This governs every non-typographic, non-verbal token: the mark, iconography,
illustration, motion signatures, empty states, loaders, success and error
states, favicons, app icons, wristbands, stickers.

Read this before drawing anything.

## The sign

The Evento Livre sign is an **index** of one moment: **the instant a person
enters an event by showing their phone to be validated.**

In Peirce's terms:

- An **icon** means by resemblance (a drawing of a phone means "phone").
- A **symbol** means by convention (a red octagon means "stop" because we
  agreed it does).
- An **index** means by pointing at something that actually happens (smoke
  points at fire, a footprint points at a foot).

Our sign is indexical. It does not merely resemble a hand and a phone. It
points at the moment of entry, the way a footprint points at someone having
walked here. Whoever sees it should feel "this is the moment I get in".

## What the sign depicts

Only two things:

1. **The attendee's hand.** One hand, the attendee's own. It is the subject.
2. **The attendee's phone.** Held in that hand, presented forward, screen
   toward whoever is validating.

The hand and the phone together form the gesture of presenting: "here I am,
let me in". That gesture is the sign.

## What the sign never depicts

- The event employee, their hand, their device, their scanner, their uniform.
- The scanner beam, the QR code as hero, the barcode, the "beep".
- Turnstiles, gates, ropes, doors, wristbands, paper tickets, stubs.
- A checkmark on its own. It is generic and it belongs to everyone.
- A crowd, a stage, a venue. The sign is about the person, not the place.

The employee is deliberately absent because the sign belongs to the attendee.
Evento Livre stands where the employee stands, but it is not the employee.
The message is: **Evento Livre is here to validate you into the event.**
The reader is the "you".

## What the sign must be

- **Intuitive.** A stranger with no caption reads it as "I show my phone,
  I get in" in under a second.
- **Simple.** Few strokes. Reduces to one colour and to 16px without losing
  the gesture. If the phone screen needs detail to read, the drawing is wrong.
- **Warm.** It is a hand, so it is human. Do not make it a diagram.
- **Forward-facing.** The phone faces out. The gesture is offered, not
  withheld. Openness is what "livre" means.

## How every non-verbal token traces back to it

| Token family | Must refer to |
|---|---|
| Mark / app icon | The gesture itself: hand presenting phone. |
| Iconography | Same stroke weight, corner logic and hand-drawn warmth as the mark. Icons for "ticket", "entry", "check-in", "validated" reuse the gesture, never a QR or a gate. |
| Illustration | Scenes built around the attendee at the moment of entry. First person or over the attendee's shoulder. The validator is out of frame. |
| Motion signature (B2C) | The phone being raised and presented. The reveal of the ticket is the hand offering it. |
| Success state | "You are in." Derived from the gesture completing, not from a checkmark appearing. |
| Loading / empty | The hand before the phone is raised: readiness, not waiting. |
| Error state | The gesture interrupted, still the attendee's hand, never a red X from an authority. |

B2B surfaces use the same visual grammar (stroke, corner, warmth) but show
the gesture less often. Producers see the mark; they do not need the story
retold on every screen.

## The test

Before any non-verbal token ships, answer these:

1. Does it point at the moment of entry?
2. Is the attendee's hand and phone the only actor?
3. Would a stranger read it as "I show my phone, I get in"?
4. Does it still read at 16px in one colour?

Four yes, or redraw.
